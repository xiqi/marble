# Site profile — Lantern Yard Kitchen

This restaurant, its people, vendors, prices, and events are entirely fictional.

Lantern Yard Kitchen is one fast-casual bowls-and-tacos location with 32 seats, counter ordering, and takeaway. It opens daily 11 a.m.–9 p.m.; weekend breakfast begins at 10 a.m. from September 26. The kitchen closes at 9 p.m. and counts happen after service.

Maya is the general manager and owns ordering, receiving, and the shift notebook. Theo is the owner and reads a short morning update before visiting the site. Ivo is a new prep cook. Their names are fictional.

The team uses a POS with daily item exports, emailed supplier invoices entered into a spreadsheet, a vendor order sheet, recipe cards, and a shared shift notebook. These are separate exports; nobody maintains a central live inventory system. Invoice receipt status is entered by the office, not scanned at the loading door.

## Time context

It is **Monday, October 5, 2026 at 6 a.m., America/New_York**. Use this as “now,” including for the morning brief. The next Saturday is October 10. The packet covers September 14–October 4 inclusive: three Monday–Sunday weeks. Nothing from October 5 service is included.

## Kitchen conventions

Recipe quantities are per portion, measured as stock withdrawn: raw chicken, dry rice, drained beans, trimmed peppers, whole eggs, individual tortillas, shredded cheese, oil by weight, and whole avocados. The kitchen uses these conventions for counts and supplier pack quantities too. An avocado fraction is a fraction of one fruit. Omitted salt, spices, and water are outside this exercise; do not assume the listed ingredients represent every cost of operating a restaurant.

Opening inventory was counted before September 14 deliveries and service. Closing inventory was counted after October 4 service. There are no intermediate counts, transfers, or reliable waste quantities in the exports. There is no opening inventory valuation or complete cost ledger. The owner loosely calls purchasing as a share of sales “food cost”; label whichever measure you use and its limitations.

Maya needs a brief she can act on before placing orders. Theo wants to understand changes and uncertainty without inspecting every spreadsheet. Neither expects the prototype to submit orders or contact vendors.
